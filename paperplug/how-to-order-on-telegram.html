Before you type anything

Three things to have ready, because missing any of them turns a two-minute quote into a two-day conversation:

  • The document you actually need. Not "an ID" — a state ID, a Real ID, a licence, a passport. The use decides it. See the price list for what each one is for.
  • Your details, written out. Full name, date of birth, address. Write them once in a notes app and paste. Typing them live is where spelling errors come from.
  • A photo that will survive a crop. Spec below.

A filtered photo produces a card that looks airbrushed next to a real one. This is the single most common quality complaint, and it is the buyer's fault nine times out of ten.

Write one complete message

Not "hi". Not "you there?". One message with six lines:

  1. Document type
  2. State or country
  3. Full name exactly as it should print
  4. Date of birth
  5. Full address, real street, no PO box
  6. Photo, two shots

That message gets a firm quote and a proof in one reply. A "hi" gets a reply that asks for six things, and you have spent a round trip learning nothing. Median first reply is 11 minutes inside hours — most of that is spent waiting for people to send a second message with the details they left out.

Reading the proof

The proof is a flattened image of your card with your details in place. It is the last cheap moment in the order. After production starts, a fix costs a reprint and a week.

Check in this order:

  • Name, character by character, including middle initial
  • Date of birth — day, month, year, and that the layout orientation matches it (vertical for under 21)
  • Expiry year — must follow that state's issue term from your issue date
  • Address — abbreviation style, apartment format, ZIP against the city
  • Class code on a licence
  • Portrait — crop, scale, position, no squashed aspect ratio
  • Star placement on a Real ID

If something is off, say which line and what it should read. "The expiry says 2031, Texas issues 8 years from 2024 so it should be 2032" is a useful message. "Looks wrong" is not.

Paying

Half up front, half on tracking. That is the only split worth accepting from a vendor you have not used. It caps your exposure at half and gives the vendor a reason to actually ship.

Pay in BTC, ETH, USDT or LTC for 5% off. Card and bank rails exist but add friction and a paper trail. Send the transaction hash in the chat the moment you have paid — do not make anyone chase it.

Red flags at the payment step: 100% up front before any proof, a wallet address that changes between messages, a demand for gift cards, and "pay now and I'll add tracking later".

After dispatch

You get dispatch photos and a tracking number. Paste the number into the carrier's own site before you pay the balance. A number that resolves to a real collection scan is proof of shipment. A number that resolves to nothing is a string.

Keep the envelope and any insert for 14 days. Reprints need the original packaging photographed in most cases, and it costs nothing to leave it in a drawer.

The six mistakes that add days

  1. Opening with "hi" and no details
  2. A filtered or overhead-angle photo
  3. Sending the DOB in the wrong format, so day and month get transposed
  4. A PO box or a fake ZIP that does not match the city
  5. Paying before the proof, then asking for a change
  6. Not reading the proof at all, then opening a dispute about a spelling that was visible
